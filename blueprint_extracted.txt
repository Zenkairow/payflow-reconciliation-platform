1. Project overview
1.1 Business problem
A fictional payments company, PayFlow, has difficulty balancing its books at month end. It has one dataset from its payment platform and another from its bank. The datasets should correspond, but differences arise from settlement timing, duplicates, rounding, refunds, missing settlements, unexpected bank entries, amount mismatches, and attribute mismatches.
The product must compare the datasets, identify the cause of each difference, preserve traceability to the source records, and explain the result to a finance or operations user.
1.2 Product objective
Build a browser-based payment reconciliation application that lets a finance operations analyst inspect platform and bank records, run a deterministic reconciliation, investigate exceptions, understand financial totals, and export results.
1.3 Primary user
Finance Operations Analyst. The user is comfortable with spreadsheets and financial operations but is not expected to understand code or reconciliation-engine internals.
1.4 Product promise
“Know what matched, what did not, and exactly why.”
1.5 Success criteria
A first-time user can open the application and run the seeded demonstration without configuration.
All four mandatory assessment scenarios are correctly detected and explained.
The application distinguishes raw source record counts, unique transaction groups, accepted matches, and exceptions.
Financial calculations are reproducible and traceable to source records.
The evaluator can inspect records, exceptions, and exports without access to the development environment.
A public deployment URL is verified in a browser before it is described as deployed.

5. Reconciliation engine specification
The engine is the highest-priority component. Implement it as a separate, testable module that does not depend on UI components. The UI must display the engine's calculated output, not its own hardcoded interpretation.
5.1 Required processing sequence
Validate input schema and normalize safe representations without altering raw source values.
Assign or verify unique source_record_id/bank_record_id values so every physical row remains traceable.
Validate required fields, dates, currency, transaction type, amount format, and status.
Group records by transaction identity and detect duplicates within each source before accepting one-to-one matches.
Validate refund records and their original payment references.
Match eligible non-duplicate records using transaction identity and required attributes.
Recognize valid cross-period settlements and classify them as timing differences where they cross the configured reporting boundary.
Classify remaining platform-only and bank-only records, while excluding records already explained by duplicate groups or other higher-priority classifications.
Calculate aggregate rounding variance separately from per-transaction matching.
Calculate financial summaries with documented inclusion/exclusion rules.
Return a structured result containing matched pairs, exception groups, validation issues, summary metrics, and traceable source IDs.
5.2 Matching rules
Transaction ID is a candidate key, not sufficient proof of a match.
Validate transaction type, merchant identity when available/required, currency, amount, and relevant date relationship.
Do not force a match when a duplicate makes the candidate ambiguous.
Do not classify a legitimate next-month settlement as missing merely because the settlement date is in the next month.
If a required attribute differs, classify according to the documented precedence rules and retain both source records.
A record must not be consumed by more than one accepted match.
All classification precedence rules must be explicit and covered by tests.

5.4 Classification precedence and overlap control
Define and document a precedence policy before coding. At minimum: invalid source records are not accepted as matches; duplicate groups are detected before matching; unmatched refunds are validated before ordinary payment matching; valid cross-period settlements are not missing; matched records are not also classified as bank-only; and duplicate counterparts must not be mislabeled as unexpected bank entries. If multiple conditions apply, select a primary classification and preserve secondary diagnostic details without inflating exception counts.
5.5 Timing treatment
Use transaction_date for platform recognition and settlement_date for bank settlement. A January 31 platform payment settled February 1 must be traceable to the corresponding bank record. The engine should identify the period difference and explain that the settlement occurred in the next month. Do not treat it as a permanently missing settlement.
5.6 Aggregate rounding treatment
Mandatory synthetic case: three platform records of ₹10.004 each and three corresponding bank records of ₹10.00 each. Platform raw total = ₹30.012; bank raw total = ₹30.000; raw difference = ₹0.012. Each pair may display as ₹10.00 at two decimal places. The engine must preserve raw precision, show the aggregate variance, and avoid creating missing-payment exceptions. Clearly label sub-paise precision as a synthetic testing assumption, not a claim about any particular bank's actual processing.
5.7 Duplicate treatment
Mandatory case: one platform transaction appears twice and its bank counterpart appears once. Preserve all three source rows, flag the platform duplicate group, prevent an ambiguous one-to-one match, do not double-count the duplicated amount in adjusted totals, and do not label the single bank counterpart as an unrelated unexpected bank entry. Explain the chosen treatment in the financial summary.
5.8 Refund validation
A refund must reference an existing valid original payment. Validate the original_transaction_id, transaction type, merchant/currency consistency where applicable, and the relationship between refund and original payment. A refund referencing a nonexistent original payment must be UNMATCHED_REFUND. Do not count it as a valid refund in the validated refund subtotal; retain it in unresolved/source totals and explain the exclusion.
5.9 Decimal-safe money
Do not use binary floating-point arithmetic as the authoritative representation for money.
Use a decimal library if appropriate, or store amounts as integer units at a defined precision (for example, thousandths of a rupee for this synthetic test) with explicit parsing and formatting.
Document supported precision and rounding policy.
Do not round before aggregate calculations.
Display INR to two decimal places in ordinary UI surfaces while exposing raw precision in the rounding explanation/details.
Use a configurable amount tolerance and document its purpose; never silently suppress a difference.
5.10 Financial summary definitions
Define the sign convention and inclusion rules before displaying totals. Recommended convention: source amounts are positive magnitudes and transaction_type determines direction; payments increase net and refunds reduce net. Display gross payments and gross refunds separately for each source. Show net platform and net bank amounts, raw difference, matched totals, duplicate-excluded amounts, and unresolved exception amounts as separate measures. Never mix a raw source total with an adjusted valid-match total without labeling it.
The application must show a short methodology note explaining how duplicates, unmatched refunds, timing differences, and rounding variance affect each figure.
